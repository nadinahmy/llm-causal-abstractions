Running example for everything below:

so $a \sim b$, but $a \not\sim c$ and $b \not\sim c$.

- The free algebra $A^*$ is just the set of all finite sequences of intervention, and $\cdot$ is concatenation. A sequence like $a \cdot c \cdot b$ is a **to-do list**, executed left to right: set $X$ to 3, then $Y$ to 7, then $X$ to 5.
- Problem: $A^*$ distinguishes too much. The lists $a \cdot c \cdot b$ and $c \cdot b$ obviously do the same thing to any model (the $X:=3$ gets overwritten anyway), but as sequences they're different objects.
	- In other words : The problem A* has is that it's _too_ fine-grained. The lists a·c·b and c·b clearly do the same thing to any model (the "X:=3" gets overwritten by "X:=5" anyway), but as sequences they're different objects. ==Definitions 17–18 exist to fix exactly this==: to say when two to-do lists are "morally the same."
### Definition 17 — the rewrite rules ($\approx$) :
- $\approx$ is generated by two local rules, which are just the two properties of hard interventions (Def 16) transplanted onto sequences:
	1. **Swap.** If $x \not\sim y$ then $x \cdot y \approx y \cdot x$. Edits to different variables can be reordered.
	2. **Delete.** If $x \sim y$ then $y \cdot x \approx x$. A later edit to the same variable makes the earlier one irrelevant. E.g. $a \cdot b \approx b$.
The phrase _"smallest congruence extending (1)"_ decodes as:
- **congruence** = equivalence relation that respects concatenation, so the rules can be applied _anywhere inside_ a longer sequence ($\alpha \approx \beta \Rightarrow \gamma\cdot\alpha\cdot\delta \approx \gamma\cdot\beta\cdot\delta$)
- **smallest** = two sequences are $\approx$-equivalent if and only if you can get from one to the other by a _finite chain_ of swap/delete steps. Nothing else counts.
> [!example] 
> $a \cdot c \cdot b ;\xrightarrow{\text{swap } a,c}; c \cdot a \cdot b ;\xrightarrow{\text{delete } a}; c \cdot b$
> 
> So $a \cdot c \cdot b \approx c \cdot b$, matching the intuition.
### Definition 18 — normal form
Chains of rewrites are painful to reason about directly, so define a deterministic cleanup:
- **Collapse**: delete every intervention that has a $\sim$-equivalent intervention somewhere to its right. Plainly: _keep only the last edit per variable._
- **Sort**: fix an arbitrary order $\lessdot$ on the $\sim$-classes (i.e. on the variables) once and for all, and sort the survivors by it.
$$\text{normal form of } \alpha = \mathrm{Sort}(\mathrm{Collapse}(\alpha))$$

On the example: $a \cdot c \cdot b \xrightarrow{\text{Collapse}} c \cdot b \xrightarrow{\text{Sort},, X \lessdot Y} b \cdot c$.

Two things worth noting:
- $\lessdot$ is completely arbitrary — its only job is uniqueness, like alphabetizing
- After Collapse there's at most one atom per variable left, so sorting is unambiguous. Hence "_the_ normal form".
## Lemma 19 — rewrites and cleanup agree

$$\alpha \approx \beta \iff \mathrm{Sort}(\mathrm{Collapse}(\alpha)) = \mathrm{Sort}(\mathrm{Collapse}(\beta))$$

- **($\approx \subseteq \equiv$)** where $\equiv$ means "same normal form". Check $\equiv$ is a congruence extending the two rules: swapping different-variable atoms changes neither which atom is last per variable nor the sorted result; deleting an overwritten atom is literally what Collapse does anyway. But $\approx$ was _defined_ as the smallest such congruence, so $\approx \subseteq \equiv$. This is the whole reason the word "smallest" is in Def 17 — it makes this direction free.
- **($\equiv \subseteq \approx$)** every sequence can be rewritten into its own normal form, i.e. $\mathrm{Collapse}(c) \approx c$ and $\mathrm{Sort}(c) \approx c$. Intuition: delete rule kills the overwritten atoms; the survivors all target distinct variables, so they pairwise commute, and the swap rule bubbles them into $\lessdot$-order. Then if $\alpha, \beta$ share normal form $N$: $\alpha \approx N \approx \beta$.
> [!tip] Sanity check Any sequence with normal form $b \cdot c$ — e.g. $(X:=9)\cdot(X:=5)\cdot(Y:=7)$, or $(Y:=7)\cdot(X:=5)$ — is $\approx$-equivalent to $a \cdot c \cdot b$.
## Theorem 20 — the representation theorem

**The quotient $A^*/!\approx$**: stop distinguishing equivalent sequences. Elements are classes $[\alpha]_\approx$; by Lemma 19, think of each class as being _named by its normal form_. The operation is

$$[\alpha]_\approx \odot [\beta]_\approx = [\alpha \cdot \beta]_\approx$$

i.e. concatenate any representatives, take the class. ($\approx$ being a congruence is exactly what makes this independent of the representatives chosen.)

**Claim**: $(A^*/!\approx, \odot)$ is an intervention algebra — per Def 15, isomorphic to $(\Phi, \circ)$, the hard interventions under composition, _for some signature_. Note the "some": $A$ was an arbitrary set, no causal model in sight. The theorem manufactures a signature that fits.
### Proof steps

**1. Build the signature out of $A$ itself.** This is the clever move. Variables := the $\sim$-equivalence classes of $A$. Values of each variable := the members of that class. In the example: variable $\mathcal{X} = {a, b}$ with values literally being the atoms $a, b$; variable $\mathcal{Y} = {c}$. Under this reading each atom _is_ an atomic hard intervention: $a$ = "set $\mathcal{X}$ to the value $a$".
**2. Define $\iota^*: A^* \to \Phi$** by $\iota^*(x_1 \cdots x_n) = x_1 \circ \dots \circ x_n$: read the to-do list, compose it as actual hard interventions on the manufactured signature.
**3. Equation (2), the hinge:** $$\iota^*(\alpha) = \iota^*(\mathrm{Collapse}(\alpha)) = \iota^*(\mathrm{Sort}(\mathrm{Collapse}(\alpha)))$$
Why: actual hard interventions genuinely satisfy commutativity and left-annihilativity (Def 16), so the syntactic cleanup is invisible once you interpret the sequence as a real composed intervention.

**4. $\iota([\alpha]_\approx) := \iota^*(\alpha)$ is an isomorphism.**
- _Well-defined_: $\alpha \approx \beta$ ⟹ same normal form (L19) ⟹ both map to $\iota^*$ of that normal form (Eq 2).
- _Surjective_: a finite hard intervention sets finitely many variables to values; each single assignment is an atom, so the whole thing is $\iota^*$ of some sequence.
- _Injective_: different normal forms ⟹ look at the $\lessdot$-least variable where they disagree; the composed interventions genuinely act differently on it, so they're different functions.
- _Homomorphism_: the 8-line equality chain looks bad but every line is one of {def of $\odot$, def of def. of $\iota / \iota^\ast$ , Lemma 19, Eq (2)}. Content: both $\iota([\alpha] \odot [\beta])$ and $\iota([\alpha]) \circ \iota([\beta])$ boil down to $\iota^\ast(\alpha \cdot \beta)$, approached from the two sides.
